Case study

dataWASHES

gesid / dataWASHES

Live platform ↗ Dashboard ↗ Source ↗

The WASHES workshop explores the social and human aspects of software engineering, with its proceedings published in SBC OpenLib. Finding patterns across its history means consulting individual publications, making even simple questions about the workshop's output time-consuming to answer. dataWASHES brings these publications together in a structured corpus of papers, authors, editions, research methods and citations, accessible through a public platform, an API and an interactive dashboard.

My contribution

I initially worked on backend routes, wrote tests, and helped build an early version of the dashboard. More recently, I redesigned the dashboard into an interactive interface for exploring the workshop's research output.

  • Automated corpus updates. A monthly GitHub Actions workflow collects new proceedings and uses an LLM to classify each paper's research approach and data-analysis methods from its abstract. It also refreshes citation counts, rebuilds the datasets, and opens a pull request for review before the changes reach the published data.
  • Citation collection. Citation counts previously relied on a separate .NET tool in another repository, which the workflow checked out and ran. I adapted a colleague's version to run within the project's Python pipeline. Each run now includes a citation audit report in the pull request to support review.
  • Interactive dashboard. I reorganised the dashboard into four thematic tabs, improved its responsive layout, and unified filtering across charts, indicators and the awards table. Selecting a chart element opens a modal listing the corresponding papers, their metadata and direct links to SBC OpenLib. The dashboard also supports exports in CSV, JSON, BibTeX and RIS formats, making the corpus easier to explore and reuse in research workflows. I also improved chart readability, navigation and keyboard accessibility.

Research

My research experience in software engineering has developed through collaborative empirical work on recruitment and selection, alongside an ongoing multivocal literature review.

I have contributed to qualitative research on early-career software engineers' experiences with recruitment and selection, working with an international team of researchers affiliated with institutions in Brazil, Germany, and Italy. My work has included conducting interviews, preparing recordings and transcripts, contributing to qualitative analysis, preparing figures and research artifacts, drafting manuscript sections, and organising materials used throughout the research process. These activities have involved both the practical work of preparing empirical data and participation in the analysis and reporting of findings.

I am currently working on a multivocal literature review of recruitment and selection in software engineering. This involves screening academic and grey literature, applying eligibility criteria, reviewing screening decisions, and documenting the process.
