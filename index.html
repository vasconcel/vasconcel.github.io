[01] Approach

Research orientation

Understanding how methodological choices shape what we observe, interpret, and claim about software engineering phenomena.

  1. Qualitative and empirical methodology

    My primary focus lies in research methods, especially qualitative inquiry, Reflexive Thematic Analysis, and the epistemological commitments behind empirical evidence. I am interested in how structured analytical procedures uncover patterns and mechanisms across real contexts.

  2. Evidence synthesis and Open Science

    Conducting Multivocal Literature Reviews that combine academic and grey literature under auditable protocols. I organize traceable datasets and open codebooks so analytical decisions remain inspectable.

  3. Open interdisciplinary agenda

    Recent studies examined tech recruitment as an initial empirical domain rather than a fixed thematic boundary. My agenda remains open to sociotechnical questions across developer practices, AI consequences, computing education, health systems, and open source communities.

[02] Publications

Publications and manuscripts

Peer-reviewed publications, manuscripts under revision, and ongoing secondary studies.

  1. Published 2026

    Constructive Patterns for Human-Centered Tech Hiring

    Proceedings of the 19th International Conference on Cooperative and Human Aspects of Software Engineering (CHASE 2026), co-located with ICSE 2026.

    A. A. Araújo, G. Vasconcelos, M. Wyrich, M. T. Baldassarre, P. Guenes, and M. Kalinowski.

    Qualitative interview study with 22 early-career software engineers examining over 470 online hiring experiences through Applicant Attribution-Reaction Theory (AART), synthesizing 22 constructive patterns for human-centered recruitment practices.

    DOI ↗ arXiv ↗ Study page ↗
  2. Minor revision 2026

    Anti-patterns in Online Recruitment and Selection Experienced by Early-Career Software Engineers

    Under second-round review at Empirical Software Engineering (EMSE-D-25-00839R1).

    G. Vasconcelos, A. A. Araújo, M. Wyrich, and M. Kalinowski.

    Qualitative investigation of negative online hiring experiences reported by 22 early-career Brazilian software engineers, identifying 25 anti-patterns across 10 analytical themes through Reflexive Thematic Analysis and AART, accompanied by an open codebook and interactive web panel.

    Zenodo dataset ↗ Project page ↗
  3. In progress 2026

    Multivocal Literature Review on Recruitment and Selection in Software Engineering

    Ongoing secondary study combining academic and grey literature.

    Multi-source evidence synthesis mapping empirical and practitioner literature on software engineering recruitment and selection, currently undergoing screening verification and protocol auditing.
