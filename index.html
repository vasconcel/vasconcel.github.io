Case 01 — Research data infrastructure

dataWASHES

gesid / dataWASHES · 2016–2026

Live platform ↗ Dashboard ↗ Source ↗

The WASHES workshop explores the social and human aspects of software engineering, with its proceedings published in SBC OpenLib. Finding patterns across its history means consulting individual publications, making even simple questions about the workshop's output time-consuming to answer. dataWASHES brings these publications together in a structured corpus of papers, authors, editions, research methods, and citations, accessible through a public platform, an API, and an interactive dashboard.

The corpus is maintained through a monthly GitHub Actions workflow that scrapes the official WASHES proceedings in SBC OpenLib, classifies six methodological fields from each paper's abstract with Groq's Llama 3.3 70B, collects citation counts from Google Scholar through ScraperAPI, regenerates the JSON datasets, and opens a pull request. Maintainers review the pull request before the published data is updated. Methodological classification is LLM-assisted and should not be read as a fully validated classification.

My contribution

dataWASHES is a collaborative, open-source project. I have contributed at different stages, from its backend and tests to citation collection and the dashboard.

  1. 01

    Backend, tests and early dashboard

    I worked on the platform's backend routes, wrote tests, and contributed to an early version of the dashboard.

  2. 02

    Citation collection

    Citation counts previously relied on a separate .NET tool in another repository, which the workflow checked out and ran. I adapted a colleague's implementation to run inside the project's Python pipeline, with credit to its original author. Each run now includes a citation audit report in the pull request to support review.

  3. 03

    Interactive dashboard

    I reorganised the dashboard into four thematic tabs, improved its responsive layout, and unified filtering across charts, indicators and the awards table. Selecting a chart element opens a modal listing the corresponding papers, their metadata and direct links to SBC OpenLib. The dashboard supports exports in CSV, JSON, BibTeX and RIS formats. I also improved chart readability, navigation and keyboard accessibility.

Case 02 — Empirical study

Constructive Patterns for Human-Centered Tech Hiring

CHASE 2026 · co-located with ICSE 2026

Artifact ↗ Preprint ↗ Conference ↗

Online recruitment and selection (R&S) is often the first contact between early-career software engineers and the tech industry. Prior work has documented recurring flaws; less is known about the practices associated with positive candidate experiences. Guided by Applicant Attribution-Reaction Theory, the study analysed interviews with early-career software engineers and produced a catalogue of constructive patterns covering job advertisements, feedback, interaction, and the framing of the process as a two-way exchange.

My contribution

I am the second author. My contribution included conducting interviews, preparing and analysing qualitative data, contributing to pattern development, and preparing research materials and manuscript sections.

The 22 patterns and the study's conclusions are collective results of the author team; they are not individual findings.
